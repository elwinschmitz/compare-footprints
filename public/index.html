Why 100 km? Cars show one tenth of the report's ~1,000 km trip (one occupant). Flying shows a 100 passenger-km share of the Amsterdam–Geneva estimate: 110 kg × 100 / 740 km ≈ 15 kg, including non-CO₂ effects (radiative forcing, RF). This is an allocation of that route's footprint, not a real 100 km flight or a prediction for other routes.

Three assumptions worth knowing

  • Grid: NL 300 g CO₂e/kWh for home, office, EV and warehouses; data centres 400 g CO₂e/kWh (global average, location-based).
  • Primary energy: electricity × 1.9; fuels × 1.2 (well-to-tank uplift). Food energy is cumulative energy demand (CED), which is already primary.
  • Flight: non-CO₂ effects (radiative forcing, RF) are included in the table (DESNZ method, ~1.9×); the value without RF is in brackets.

These figures are rough, AI-assisted (!) estimates intended to put orders of magnitude in perspective, not precise measurements. Read the full report for calculations, sources, assumptions and limitations.

Embodied emissions of devices, vehicles, servers and buildings are excluded. Some sources remain unverified, as documented in the report.

The making of this page: the logged AI work across this session, including planning, generation, design revisions and checks, is estimated at about 0.26 kWh of server-side energy (rough range: 0.09–0.8 kWh). This uses recorded token counts and the report's per-token method as a proxy, not measured energy or coefficients verified for the models used. Excludes local devices, browser/tool execution and subsequent requests.
